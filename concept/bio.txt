I am a PhD student at the Vermont Complex Systems Institute at the University of Vermont. My research focuses on my fellow computational social scientists who study misinformation. Before becoming a PhD student, I was a software developer in the tech industry for almost 15 years. At the same time, I have been involved in political communication in electoral and labor politics, and am the currently-serving, elected Communications Chair of a political organization. 

Misinformation research sits at the intersection of surveillance data and worldwide political destabilization, but it has no theory of either. It takes the former for granted, and the latter it considers pathological, a deviation from "normal," in almost the same way that a lie is a deviation from the truth. Worse still, the field is wholly detached from real-world politics, e.g., it shows no interest in the actual theory of those who manipulate public opinion, e.g., marketing, public relations, etc.

I am applying for this conference because my attempts to engage with "misinformation" in my own field are generally rejected, quashed by peer review, or ignored at conferences.
